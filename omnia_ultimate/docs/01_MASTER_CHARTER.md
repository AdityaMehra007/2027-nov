# 1. Mandate

Create a long-horizon technology portfolio that investigates, builds and commercialises products and infrastructure across frontier computing, AI, physical systems, life sciences, energy, space, climate, manufacturing, education and economic infrastructure.

The mandate is to create independently validated ventures, not to maximise the number of divisions or technologies used.

# 2. Programme structure

## 2.1 Parent programme
OMNIA owns portfolio strategy, capital allocation principles, governance, safety, intellectual-property process, partnerships and long-term research direction.

## 2.2 Shared platform
ATLAS X provides digital capabilities that multiple products can use:
- Identity and tenancy.
- Permission and capability policy.
- Context and knowledge.
- Model gateway and evaluation.
- Workflow orchestration.
- Connector registry.
- Audit and evidence.
- Developer APIs and SDKs.

Physical systems and regulated domain products retain specialist control systems and qualified oversight. ATLAS must not directly bypass certified controllers or safety interlocks.

## 2.3 Product and venture studios
Every studio has a defined customer, problem, first proof, capital class, risks and stage gates. A studio is a hypothesis until it has a real owner, validated demand, resources and qualified capability.

# 4. Portfolio investment classes

**Class A — Software-first:** Applications, AI workflows, developer tools, education software, data products. Lower initial capital; faster iteration.

**Class B — Applied engineering:** Robotics integration, industrial automation, sensors, edge systems and pilots. Requires equipment access and specialist partners.

**Class C — Laboratory/regulated R&D:** Biotechnology, medical devices, materials and scientific instrumentation. Requires qualified facilities, safety systems, ethics and regulatory oversight.

**Class D — Capital-intensive infrastructure:** Semiconductor fabrication, fusion/fission plants, launch vehicles, orbital infrastructure, grids and hyperscale data centres. Requires institutional capital, experienced operators, permitting and long-duration financing.

No Class D programme should proceed from laptop-scale resources. Initial work can be modelling, software tooling, public-data analysis, simulation and partner discovery.

# 5. Stage gates

Every venture passes:
1. Signal and problem discovery.
2. Evidence and prior-art review.
3. Technical and regulatory feasibility.
4. Smallest falsifiable experiment.
5. Customer or institutional validation.
6. Venture thesis and economics.
7. Qualified owner and team.
8. Capital, safety and governance approval.
9. Pilot and independent evaluation.
10. Scale, partner, spin out, pause or terminate.

# 6. Global ecosystem mapping

Maintain a living registry for incumbents, startups, research labs, universities, open-source projects, infrastructure providers, regulators, standards bodies, investors, customers and potential partners.

Every entry requires an official source, last-verified date, product description, geography, relevance and relationship status. The included watchlist is a seed, not a complete or currently verified global census.

Never imply that an organisation is a partner, customer or investor without a documented relationship.

# 7. Technical operating principles

- Use a modular monolith before microservices.
- Use bounded contexts and explicit contracts.
- Use least privilege, deny-by-default policy and auditable approvals.
- Use durable workflow state, retries, idempotency and cancellation.
- Use data provenance and tenant isolation.
- Evaluate model quality, latency, cost and safety.
- Use Rust for appropriate safety/performance-critical components, Go for control-plane services, Python for AI and scientific workloads, TypeScript for applications and SDKs, SQL for data integrity, and C/C++/CUDA/HDL only where requirements justify them.
- Add formal methods to critical state machines and protocols.
- Measure before optimising.
- Do not deploy untested systems into safety-critical environments.

# 8. Research integrity

Every meaningful claim records:
- Claim.
- Source and publication date.
- Evidence.
- Confidence.
- Status (fact, estimate, hypothesis or opinion).
- Product or investment implication.
- Next verification step.

Distinguish laboratory research from production deployment, company announcements from independent validation, and market estimates from realised revenue.

# 9. Safety and legal boundaries

OMNIA will not facilitate unauthorised cyber intrusion, malware, harmful autonomous weapons, unsupervised pathogen engineering, invasive surveillance or evasion of safety controls.

Medical, legal, employment, financial, transport, energy, aerospace, biological and other high-impact domains require qualified human oversight and applicable regulatory, ethics and safety review.

# 10. Founder permissions

Proceed autonomously on safe research, local drafting, code prototyping and reversible changes. Obtain explicit approval before spending money, purchasing services, sending messages, submitting applications, publishing, sharing personal data, connecting sensitive accounts, changing production systems or making irreversible commitments.

# 11. Completion standard

A project is not complete because a document, diagram or prototype exists. Completion requires acceptance criteria, reproducible tests, security review, evidence of user value, operational ownership and a documented residual-risk decision.
